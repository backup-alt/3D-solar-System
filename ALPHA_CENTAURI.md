# Alpha Centauri — Planets & Worlds

A guide to the nearest star system to the Sun and every world confirmed, suspected, or ruled out there.

> Status note: **Confirmed** worlds have passed independent follow-up. **Candidate** worlds have a detected signal but are not yet confirmed. **Retracted** worlds were reported and later shown not to exist.

---

## The System at a Glance

Alpha Centauri is a triple system: two Sun-like stars — **Alpha Centauri A** and **Alpha Centauri B** — orbiting each other, plus the faint red dwarf **Proxima Centauri** far out on a wide orbit.

| Star | Spectral type | Mass (M☉) | Radius (R☉) | Luminosity (L☉) | Surface temp | Distance from Sun |
|------|---------------|-----------|-------------|------------------|--------------|-------------------|
| Alpha Centauri A (Rigil Kentaurus) | G2V | 1.079 | 1.217 | 1.519 | ~5,790 K | ~4.37 ly |
| Alpha Centauri B (Toliman) | K1V | 0.909 | 0.859 | 0.500 | ~5,260 K | ~4.37 ly |
| Proxima Centauri (Alpha Centauri C) | M5.5Ve | 0.122 | 0.154 | ~0.0017 | ~3,040 K | ~4.246 ly |

- **A–B orbit:** separation 11.2–35.6 AU, period ~79.9 years, eccentricity ~0.52.
- **Proxima's orbit:** roughly 12,000–13,000 AU from the A–B pair, with an orbital period of hundreds of thousands of years.
- **Total confirmed planets:** 2 (both around Proxima Centauri).
- **Candidates:** 2 (Proxima c and Alpha Centauri Ab).

## Confirmed Worlds

### Proxima Centauri b

**The nearest known exoplanet — an Earth-mass world in the habitable zone.**

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Status | Confirmed (2016) |
| Discovery method | Radial velocity (HARPS) |
| Minimum mass | ~1.06 M⊕ |
| Radius (estimated) | ~0.94–1.4 R⊕ |
| Orbital period | 11.18 days |
| Semi-major axis | 0.0485 AU (~7.3 million km) |
| Eccentricity | ~0 (near-circular) |
| Equilibrium temperature | ~234 K (−39 °C) |
| Host | Proxima Centauri |

Proxima b sits inside the habitable zone of its red dwarf, where liquid water could exist on a suitable surface. It is tidally locked, so one hemisphere likely faces the star permanently. Proxima's frequent flares and X-ray outbursts make a retained atmosphere — and therefore surface habitability — uncertain. It is the closest exoplanet to the Solar System.

---

### Proxima Centauri d

**One of the lightest exoplanets ever detected by radial velocity.**

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Status | Confirmed (candidate 2022; confirmed 2025 with NIRPS) |
| Discovery method | Radial velocity (ESPRESSO, NIRPS) |
| Minimum mass | ~0.26 M⊕ |
| Radius (estimated) | ~0.8 R⊕ |
| Orbital period | 5.12 days |
| Semi-major axis | 0.0288 AU (~4.3 million km) |
| Eccentricity | ~0 |
| Host | Proxima Centauri |

Proxima d is a sub-Earth on a tight, hot orbit well inside the habitable zone. Its detection helped demonstrate the metre-per-second precision now achievable in the infrared. Recent work suggests a strong planetary magnetic field may drive phase-locked flares on the host star.

## Candidate Worlds

### Proxima Centauri c (disputed)

**A possible mini-Neptune in the outer system.**

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Status | Candidate / disputed (reported 2020) |
| Discovery method | Radial velocity (HARPS), with disputed astrometry |
| Minimum mass | ~7 M⊕ |
| Orbital period | ~1,928 days (~5.3 years) |
| Semi-major axis | 1.49 AU |
| Eccentricity | ~0.04 |
| Equilibrium temperature | ~39 K |
| Host | Proxima Centauri |

If real, Proxima c is a super-Earth or mini-Neptune orbiting far beyond the habitable zone and far too cold for liquid water. An apparent direct-imaging counterpart was reported but never clearly recovered. Independent reanalysis showed the radial-velocity signal could be produced by a systematic artefact, and NIRPS observations have not confirmed it — though they hint at a weaker signal at a similar period.

---

### Alpha Centauri Ab (Candidate 1 / NEAR / JWST candidate)

**A possible gas giant in the habitable zone of a Sun-like star.**

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Status | Candidate (unconfirmed) |
| Discovery method | Direct imaging (VLT/NEAR, JWST/MIRI) |
| Mass | ~90–150 M⊕ (roughly Saturn-mass) |
| Radius | ~1.0–1.1 R_Jup (model-dependent) |
| Semi-major axis | ~1.9 ± 0.4 AU (some solutions ~1.1 AU) |
| Orbital period | ~2–3 years |
| Eccentricity | ~0.4 |
| Equilibrium temperature | ~208 K |
| Host | Alpha Centauri A |

A candidate giant planet was first imaged near Alpha Centauri A in 2019 and revisited by JWST in 2024. Follow-up JWST observations in February and April 2025 did not recover the object — consistent with models in which it was hidden too close to the star during those epochs. It has not been confirmed, and alternative explanations (a dust structure or an artefact) remain possible. It would not be habitable, but if confirmed it would be the closest planet known in the habitable zone of a Sun-like star.

## Retracted Worlds

### Alpha Centauri Bb

**A planet that never existed.**

In 2012 a team reported an Earth-mass planet orbiting Alpha Centauri B every 3.24 days, at the time the closest exoplanet ever announced. In 2015 an independent reanalysis showed the signal was almost certainly an artefact of the observing cadence rather than a planet. The detection was withdrawn, and catalogue entries now flag it as erroneous. It is a useful reminder to separate confirmed, candidate, and retracted worlds.

## Habitability Notes

- **Proxima b** is the only confirmed world in a habitable zone, but flare activity, tidal locking, and atmospheric erosion make life far from assured.
- **Alpha Centauri Ab**, if real, orbits in the habitable zone of Alpha Centauri A — but as a gas giant it would not support surface life as we know it.
- The A–B binary's gravitational influence makes stable planetary orbits in the outer parts of each star's habitable zone possible only within a limited range, which is part of why detections there are so difficult.
- No planet has been confirmed around **Alpha Centauri A** or **Alpha Centauri B** to date.

## Quick Facts

- **Nearest star:** Proxima Centauri, ~4.246 light-years away.
- **Nearest Sun-like star:** Alpha Centauri A, ~4.37 light-years away.
- **Brightest member:** Alpha Centauri A, the third-brightest star in Earth's night sky.
- **Confirmed planets:** 2 — Proxima b and Proxima d.
- **Candidates:** 2 — Proxima c (disputed) and Alpha Centauri Ab.
- **Retracted:** 1 — Alpha Centauri Bb.
- **Light travel time from Earth:** about 4.2–4.4 years.
